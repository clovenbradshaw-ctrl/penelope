The essay

Memory has two faces, and the west has known both. The classical face is the waxen tablet: “The waxen tablet of the memory which was once capable of receiving 'true thoughts and clear impressions' becomes hard and crowded; there is not room for the accumulations of a long life (Theaet.”, and “there is not room for the accumulations of a long life (Theaet.” — a hardening, an exchange. “The student, as years advance, rather makes an exchange of knowledge than adds to his stores.”. Its remedy is not a method but the thousand small acts of self-education — “For self-education consists in a {ccxi} thousand things, commonplace in themselves,--in adding to what we are by nature something of what we are not; in learning to see ourselves as others see us; in judging, not by opinion, but by the evidence of facts; in seeking out the society of superior minds; in a study of l”, “in learning to see ourselves as others see us; in judging, not by opinion, but by the evidence of facts; in seeking out the society of superior minds; in a study of lives and writings of great men; in observation of the world and character; in receiving kindly the natural influence of different times of life; in an”. The modern face is working memory: what the old table called crowding, the new research calls maintenance in the “prefrontal cortex (PFC) function is that single neurons within the PFC maintain representations of task-relevant stimuli in working memory.”, where “working memory.” “maintenance per se .”. The wax hardens; the cortex maintains. Both are the same admission: memory is work, not storage — and what saves it, ancient and modern alike, is the exchange of knowledge for knowledge, the unhistoric act of looking outward, judging by evidence, calling forth the latent power. So the crowded tablet rests, and the maintained trace is released.
